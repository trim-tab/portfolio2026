
**To Whom It May Concern:**

Are you familiar with this idea of Umwelt?  In ethology-- the idea that organisms within the same ecosystem would have entirely different experiences.  I'm not certain how this concept came to into my vernacular, but I can't stop thinking about it.  The idea that one person's existential crisis might just be a mundane Tuesday afternoon for the other party.  

I couldn't put my finger on what exactly was happening, but then I remembered a discussion I was having with an engineer that, quicker that I could realize, became a disagreement that, given room to breathe, burst near instaneously into an argument.  It was a lesson that being right and seeing an argument through are two separate things.

It was only days later when he found information using pi units to solve complicated problems that he conceded the point.  That he had to look for a reason to have that be useful before he could think it could be possible.

Our discussion was about units. Say you took 1 and made it 1.2 it seemed to me entirely plausible that there could be times that that was useful. I proposed that there could be an instance where adjusting to fractional units could be possible. He dug in taking an aggressively, defensive stance that seemed disproportionate to the topic at hand.  I couldn't understand why he was getting so upset.  Then I saw it: I was attacking a cornerstone of his belief system, introducing chaos into his world ruled by nice, neat integers.  

Having grown up in chaos, upon chaotic, chaos masquerading as order, there is a certain comfort I have with the chaotic that baffles those who walk through worlds of neat, tidy lines; orderly possessions, artifacts neatly tucked away behind stanchions of convention.  When I want to do orderly things, it's effort. In keeping a clean work area, I resort to knolling, straight lines, and boundaries.  When I want to be creative, I simply allow the elves to come in and muss up the place. 

This is what makes Bertrand Russell's argument "This illustrates what psychoanalysts so much emphasize--the immediately stronger hold upon us that our very early associations have than those of later times."(Russell, 1957, p.8) so compelling.  This theory that initial premises told by those we trust are stronger than empirical evidence offered by the world.  

The counterargument being anyone who comes to Christ through the testimony of others later in life.

When I looked up Tolkien and Lewis what I found most interesting was Lewis's respect for Russell writing whereas Tolkien argued for his works not to be read. From a returned prodigal's perspective this makes sense, though I cannot explain why. Perhaps the curiosity that opened the box with the deceased cat still lingers.  Whereas Tolkien kept the faith, cautioning against his texts.  If everything is good and fine and you surround yourself with nice people, why look?
